Project 01 / 07 Brown Formula Racing · FSAE

Drag Reduction System for FSAE Car

A What

  • Rear-wing drag reduction system (DRS) for the 2027 FSAE car.
  • Decreases airfoil angles of attack into a low-downforce configuration, cutting drag on straights and increasing aerodynamic efficiency.

B How

  • A double-acting pneumatic cylinder drives a system of mechanical linkages, rotating three airfoils from the default high-downforce setting to near-zero angles of attack.
  • Uses the car's existing air tank for actuation — adding performance for minimal added weight and complexity.
  • Waterjet-cut carbon fiber linkages and SLS-printed Nylon 12 clevis brackets for minimized weight.
  • Shoulder bolts and bronze bushings for low-friction pinned joints at the clevises.
  • An extension spring, airfoil centers of pressure ahead of the pivots, and a solenoid return spring default the mechanism to the closed position in the event of pneumatic or electrical failure.
  • Hand calculations and FEA validate stresses in the clevis brackets.

C Progress

  • Design finalized.
  • Extracted aero loads at peak loading condition (top speed at end of straight w/ DRS closed) using Siemens STAR-CCM+.
  • ~60% drag reduction on DRS deployment (symmetric half-car model, STAR-CCM+, residuals to 1e-7).
  • Spring return that closes DRS w/ 2.25× margin and is overcome on cylinder deployment w/ 1.89× actuation margin
  • Clevis-bracket max stresses (combined bolt preload, linkage forces, and 3g bump) validated to 2.50 safety factor in FEA study in Ansys Mechanical.
  • Shoulder bolt pivot pins validated with fatigue hand calc for infinite life.
  • Designed a test rig utilizing the team's jig table to test actuator stroke before installing full mechanism on car.
  • Carbon fiber and SLS Nylon 12 clevis brackets done, and will be ordered post bench testing.
  • Targeting full rear-wing implementation by the end of December, before winter break.
  • Teaching six new BFR members SolidWorks and mechanical design through weekly CAD hours, and helping them design their own DRS test rigs.

Project 02 / 07 Brown University · DesignXHealth

Pinch Valve Prototype

A What

  • A motorized pinch-valve mechanism that regulates dialysate inflow and outflow to the patient — built as a prototype for a pediatric dialysis machine.

B How

  • A stepper motor and lead screw convert rotational motion into smooth, precise linear motion.
  • The linear actuator drives a clamp to specific positions, achieving precise fluid flow rates by varying how much a tube is pinched.
  • A removable lid and base plate let the motor be taken out for maintenance or replacement.
  • A machined acetal lead-screw nut and linear ball bearing achieve zero clamp wobble.

C Progress

  • Full prototype designed, 3D-printed, and assembled.
  • Acetal lead-screw nut and linear ball bearing achieve zero clamp wobble.
  • Currently breadboard testing the stepper motor to test repeatability of clamp positions.
  • Initial bench runs showed stepper stutter on A4988 drivers. Testing with TMC2209s instead for more reliable motion w/ UART current limiting.
  • Next: test tubing with fluid to pair target positions with specific flow rates.

Project 03 / 07

Potentiometer-Controlled Electric Linear Actuator with Gear Reduction & Position Feedback

A What

  • Stepper motor-powered linear actuator with potentiometer control, and displays live actuator extension on an LCD screen.
  • Gear reduction doubles the torque applied to the actuator stroke.

B How

  • A NEMA 17 stepper motor turns an ACME lead screw fixed to an acetal lead-screw nut for low-friction, precise linear motion.
  • A 20-tooth, 48 DP pinion on the motor shaft mates with a 40-tooth, 48 DP driven gear on the lead screw for a 2:1 gear reduction.
  • An Arduino Uno and TMC2209 driver program the stepper's motion and current limiting.
  • Two 10 kΩ potentiometers let the user manually control actuator position and speed.
  • An LCD screen displays actuator position and speed.
  • A ball bearing inside the tube assembly minimizes friction between the actuator and its housing.

C Progress

  • Parts manufactured, assembly fully constructed, and successfully achieved actuator linear travel with potentiometer speed control!
  • Screen lists live actuator speed and displacement since device turned on.
  • Next: fix stuttering at moderate to higher speeds >2,000 steps/sec for more consistent motion control.

Multi-Element Aerodynamic Wing for RC Car

A What

  • Multi-element wing that increases rear downforce on an RC car, improving handling performance.
  • Detachable mounting bracket lets the wing fit other RC car models.

B How

  • Used XFOIL to modify NACA 9712 airfoil geometry, raising the lift coefficient for the Re = 50,000 flow regime relevant to the RC car.
  • Used SolidWorks Flow Simulation to optimize element positions for maximum downforce.
  • 3D-printed the full assembly.

C Results

  • Modified NACA 9712 airfoil achieves 19.66% higher lift coefficient and 11.14% higher lift-to-drag ratio vs. the stock airfoil at a 12° angle of attack.
  • Generates 1.9 N of downforce at 40 mph.

Project 06 / 07 High School Rocketry Team

Mach 3+ Fiberglass Rocket Nose Cone

A What

  • Custom fiberglass nose cone for high-power rocket flight at Mach 3+ speeds.

B How

  • Researched supersonic nose-cone aerodynamics and settled on a conical profile.
  • Modeled a 6.5:1 conical nose cone and molds in Fusion 360.
  • 3D-printed the molds for a positive hand layup using fiberglass "sleeves."
  • Machined an Aluminum 7075 tip with an eyebolt to house the recovery system and complete the cone geometry.

C Results

  • Nose cone sits flush with the main body tube — no clearance issues, a successful fit.
  • Launched in the Mojave Desert; cleared the launch tower and held the intended trajectory through motor burnout, 4 seconds after ignition.
  • Could not recover the rocket — GPS went offline and the recovery system did not deploy.

Temperature Monitoring Device

A What

  • Arduino-controlled device that actively reads and displays room temperature.

B How

  • A TMP36 sensor reads temperature and sends a voltage signal to the Arduino.
  • The voltage signal is converted to a temperature value and displayed on the LED screen.
  • A green LED indicates the device is on; a red LED blinks and a buzzer sounds if temperature falls outside the deemed-safe range.
